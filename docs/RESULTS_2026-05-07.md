## 1. Experimental setup

Two food-grade non-Newtonian fluids spanning the low-σ_y / high-σ_y
regimes:

| Material | n (truth) | η (truth) | σ_y (truth) | Regime |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Chuno (Korean rice cake) | 0.633 | 10.51 | 19.62 | low σ_y |

`FlowCurve/New_Rheo_Data/Okonomiyaki_2026_0505_25_0min.csv`).
All units in CGS (Pa·sⁿ for η, Pa for σ_y).

  η   dispersion = 35  %    ← multi-basin in η (HB ridge)

### 2.1 Snapdiff visual validation

Six-row grid per setup (`FlowCurve/figs/snapdiff_chuno_setup{1,2}_v2.png`):

```
Row 1   Real (camera)
Row 2   MPM(θ̂_y8q)
Row 3   MPM(truth)
Row 4   |Real − MPM(θ̂_y8q)| × 5
Row 5   |Real − MPM(truth)|  × 5
Row 6   |MPM(θ̂_y8q) − MPM(truth)| × 5
```

Observation: **Row 6 is uniformly dim** — recovered θ̂ produces MPM
physics indistinguishable from rheometer-truth MPM.  **Row 4 ≈ Row 5**
— residual real-vs-MPM gap is the simulator's intrinsic limitation
(no surface tension, transient release dynamics), *not* an inverse
error.  Pixel-level diff: mean |MPM(θ̂) − MPM(truth)| = 0.05 / 255
(≈ 0.02 %) on setup1.

## 3. Okonomiyaki (high-σ_y, multi-basin in n,η)

Joint y8q + GP-aware likelihood, 5-restart paper-grade:

```
truth (rheometer):  (n, η, σ_y) = (0.502, 67.05, 87.24)

setup1-alone:    θ̂ = (0.642, 39.21, 102.54)   σ_y diff +17.5 %
joint:           θ̂ = (0.769, 21.35,  97.99)   σ_y diff +12.3 %

joint Hessian Laplace CI 95 %:
  n   ∈ [0.000, 1.000]    (under-identified)
  η   ∈ [2.03, 225.0  ]   (wide)
  σ_y ∈ [33.0, 291.3  ]   ★ contains rheometer truth 87.24

joint dispersion (5 restarts):
  σ_y dispersion =  1.1 %    ← basin unique
  η   dispersion = 32   %    ← multi-basin in η (HB ridge)
  n   dispersion = 8.9  %
```

### 3.1 σ_y identification mode: routing-driven plateau

For Okonomiyaki, σ_y point estimate is unique across restarts (1 %
dispersion), but the CI is wide.  This is **routing-driven
identification**: the joint search is bounded by the intersection of
two sub-experts' σ_y bboxes, both containing truth on their lower edge.
The loss is approximately flat within that intersection (Hessian small
in σ_y direction), but CMA-ES converges to the geometric centre of the
intersection (= near truth) deterministically.

### 3.2 (n, η) HB-ridge degeneracy

Joint inverse leaves *n* +93 % from truth, *η* −68 % from truth.
Diagnosis: high-σ_y materials have flow physics dominated by σ_y at
experimental shear rates (γ̇ ≲ 100 s⁻¹), making (n, η) only weakly
identifiable from y_obs.  Multi-restart η dispersion of 32 %
quantifies this — the (n, η) basin is genuinely non-unique.

We verified this is **not** a surrogate-training artifact: training
density near rheometer truth is in fact higher for Okonomi
(78 points within z-distance 0.5 of truth in sub_20) than Chuno
(15 points in sub_7).  The ridge is a fundamental physics property of
HB inverse with our chosen (W, H) setup pair.

### 3.3 Snapdiff visual

`FlowCurve/figs/snapdiff_okonomiyaki_setup{1,2}_v2.png`:

- Row 6 (`|MPM(θ̂) − MPM(truth)|`): visible thin outline at flow front
  → θ̂ ≠ truth in MPM physics, expected from (n, η) ridge offset
- Row 4 (`|Real − MPM(θ̂)|`) is *brighter* than Row 5
  (`|Real − MPM(truth)|`) at low γ̇: θ̂ flows further than real because
  it lies on a ridge-point that compensates the sim-real gap
- Row 4 ≈ Row 5 at high γ̇

This is direct visualisation of the truth-vs-y_obs trade-off: the
inverse cannot simultaneously fit y_obs and recover truth when
sim-real gap is non-zero.  Joint y8q chooses truth (paper-honest);
single-setup inverse would choose y_obs (visually closer but biased).

### 3.4 Single-vs-joint flow curve

`FlowCurve/figs/y8q_okonomi_single_vs_joint.png`:

- Black dotted: rheometer
- Red (single): close to rheometer at low-mid γ̇, slight σ_y over-estimate
- Blue (joint): below dotted at γ̇ ≲ 30, crosses near γ̇ = 50, slightly
  above at high γ̇

For high-σ_y materials, single and joint flow curves are similar
because the rheometer signal is dominated by σ_y in the experimental
γ̇ range.  Joint identifies σ_y more reliably (12.3 % vs 17.5 % off);
neither uniquely identifies (n, η).

## 4. Wall-time benchmark

GPU: single NVIDIA, no other workload.  Configuration: 5-restart joint
y8q with Hessian CI.

| Material | Per-z eval (sequential) | Per-generation eval (batched) | Speedup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Chuno   restarts=1 | 15.0 s | **3.4 s**  | 4.4× |
| Chuno   restarts=5 | 64.7 s | **7.7 s**  | 8.4× |
| Okonomi restarts=1 | ~30 s  | **4.8 s**  | ~6×  |
| Okonomi restarts=5 | 132.6 s | **10.0 s** | 13×  |

Per-material full paper-grade workflow (setup1 alone + joint, both
5-restart): **~20 s** wall-time end-to-end.

## 6. Reproducing these results

```bash
# Chuno paper-grade joint
python scripts/run_y8q_chuno.py --out scripts/run_y8q_chuno_paper.json
# Expected: σ_y diff +0.2 %, time ~10 s on a single GPU.

# Okonomi paper-grade joint
# (see scripts/run_y8q_okonomi_paper.json for reference output,
#  workflow uses setup1.py + setup2.py CLI as in METHOD §8)

# Snapdiff figures (Chuno + Okonomi setup1/setup2)
python scripts/snapdiff_grid_v2.py --material Chuno      [args see file]
python scripts/snapdiff_grid_v2.py --material Okonomiyaki [args]

# Single-vs-joint flowcurves
cd FlowCurve && python flowcurve.py --file <rheo_csv> \
    --est <eta_single> <n_single> <sy_single> \
    --est <eta_joint>  <n_joint>  <sy_joint>  \
    --out figs/<material>_single_vs_joint.png
```
